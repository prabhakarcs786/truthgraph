# Evidence Investigation

An investigation determines which source-backed claims apply to a question while preserving provenance, disagreement, and missing context.

## Language

**Knowledge Base**: A bounded collection of source material prepared for questions about a defined subject or purpose. It is not unrestricted internet knowledge.

**Source**: The original attributable publication behind a claim, with its publisher and known dates. A retrieval result is not itself a new authority.

**Claim**: A source-backed assertion about a subject and predicate, with a value and applicability scope. Different wording does not necessarily imply a different assertion.

**Applicability**: Whether a claim's date, version, and stated conditions cover the user's question. Missing context cannot be assumed to satisfy a condition.

**Correction**: An explicit, sourced replacement of a particular claim. It takes precedence only within its effective scope.

**Supersession**: An explicit relationship by which one claim replaces another in an applicable scope. A newer publication date alone is not supersession.

**Conflict**: Different mutually exclusive values asserted for the same subject and predicate in the same applicable scope. Different release scopes are not a conflict.

**Evidence Strength**: A description of support and unresolved gaps in the retrieved record set, not a probability or guarantee of truth.

**Synthetic Fixture**: Invented material used to test reasoning behavior, never evidence of a real-world policy or product fact.
